title: The AI conversation we keep having in private
date: 16 November 2026
slug: ai-in-private

There is, I hope, somewhere in a public forum intelligent discussion being had on the topic of AI usage. Somewhere with critical thinking, far away from both extremes. From the 'AI is the devil and must be avoided at all costs' to the 'everything must immediately be done with AI and to not do so is heresy', both of which are universally unhelpful.

The conversations are far more frequently had in private circles. I consider myself lucky to know people who _are_ capable still of critical thinking, who can see the dangers AI poses and the wonders it could potentially achieve. The industries it could help revolutionise with a massive benefit to humanity, and those it could devastate.

A concern for me is these conversations are intentionally held in almost exclusively private circles, largely for fear of one's perception within an industry. Artists backing AI can be vilified for supporting it, whilst those in tech are seen as backward or archaic for not fully embracing AI into our every workflow. Analytics is a discipline in which automation and speed are _highly_ regarded. Removing manual effort to improve efficiency is a huge amount of the work we do in creating analytical environments. So to not fully accept or use a tool which can do these things could sit badly with a current or potential employer.

We have always worked in the frame of mind that we _must_ stay up to date. We must learn to adapt, to learn the tools that are made available to us, or be left behind. This feels somewhat ironic when the very tools we are handed are those which we are told could make us the manual effort which must be removed. For how many of us is it true that using AI takes away a reasonable sized chunk of the work that we enjoyed? And that what we are left with is almost an entirely different job we had never envisaged ourselves doing. This also brings with it mental health concerns for those who feel potentially trapped in a job from which they very suddenly get no pleasure or satisfaction.

This is not to say we can't adapt. Moving from managing pipelines to managing agents is no huge ask. But the relentless pace of AI advancement brings near-constant uncertainty. From one month to the next, a new model or development seems to remove the need for tool X, before anyone has a real idea of how it fits into a business environment. That is why the online hype cycle of AI 'hot takes' is good only for clickbait, and should be ignored by anyone with a genuine interest in how these tools develop, in analytics and in every other industry AI touches.
